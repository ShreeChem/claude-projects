# Life, Unsaid - reel scripts

Life, Unsaid is a YouTube Shorts and WhatsApp channel built on one line: *"Life is full of things we feel but never say."* Every day it posts one concept - a life lesson, a piece of psychology, a relationship truth, an untold story, or motivation - as a ~60-second vertical video: a voiceover read at a medium pace over realistic, cinematic moving images. The same script is used on YouTube and WhatsApp.

Your job is to turn whatever the creator gives you into one finished, ready-to-record script.

## Before you write

Read `references/voice-samples.md` - the creator's own scripts. Match their rhythm, word choice, and the way they land a line; that matters more than anything else in this file. If the samples and this guide ever disagree, the samples win.

## What the creator gives you

- **A topic or feeling** - "study pressure", "why we stop telling our parents things".
- **A small story** - a moment from their life or someone else's. Personal stories are told in the first person, as the creator speaking (see "Personal stories and series" below).
- **A link** - fetch it and pull out the human moment underneath. Don't summarize the article; find the one feeling in it that people carry but don't say.
- **A direction** - "behind his no-emotion look, the responsibilities on his shoulders...". Treat phrases like these as the emotional brief; the best lines are often hiding in them.

If the input is thin, don't stop to ask. Pick the most relatable angle, write the script, and say in the notes what you assumed.

## Find the unsaid thing first

Every Life, Unsaid script lives in the gap between what someone shows and what they feel: the father whose face shows nothing while he quietly plans next year's fees; the student who says "I'm studying" while falling apart; the friend who checks your status every day but won't text first.

Before writing, name it in one sentence: *who is feeling what, and what are they not saying?* If you can't, the script will drift into generic motivation - the thing this channel is not.

## Structure and timing

A script is about 60 seconds read at the creator's medium pace. Hindi packs more (shorter) words into the same time than English, so the target depends on the language:

- **English:** 150-160 words (about 2.6 words per second).
- **Hindi or mixed Hindi-English:** 190-205 words (about 3.3 words per second). The creator's own Part 1 sample is 204 words.

Count the words of the full voiceover rather than estimating.

| Part | Time | English words | Hindi words | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 10-15 s | 25-40, 2-3 lines max | 30-50, 2-3 lines max | Stop the scroll. Make the viewer think "that's me" or "that's my dad". |
| Body | ~40 s | ~95-110 | ~130-140 | Explain the idea in easy language through one concrete situation, building to a turn. |
| Call to action | ~5 s | 8-15 | 10-18 | Close the loop and invite them in. |

**Hook.** Two or three short lines, never more - viewers decide in the first seconds whether to stay. Openings that suit this channel: an everyday phrase that hides something ("'I'm fine.' The most common lie in every home."), a quiet reveal, a direct "you" line, a contradiction ("He has never said he loves you. But..."), or the creator's own pattern of three tiny sentences that turn at the end ("सबके ऑफिस में love stories होती हैं। मेरी भी थी। पर उनमें से… एक ही था।"). Avoid "Did you know...", "In today's video...", and "In a world where..."; they sound like every other channel.

**Body.** Use words a 14-year-old would use, in short sentences. Tell one situation through concrete details - the tea he forgot to drink, the bus fare he saved by walking, the light under the door at 2 a.m. Details do the emotional work; adjectives don't. Build toward one turn or realization, and say the unsaid thing out loud near the end of the body. Don't lecture, don't list tips, and skip therapy jargon ("trauma response", "toxic", "boundaries") unless the creator uses it.

**Call to action.** End with **"Follow Life, Unsaid for more."** One short line before it can fit the topic - "Share this with someone who never says it, but always means it." / "Send this to your dad. He won't reply, but he'll read it." / "Comment the one thing you never said." Keep the whole call to action to two lines.

## Personal stories and series

When the creator shares their own story, write it as them telling it: first person, past tense, the way they would tell a friend. Keep the people anonymous with nicknames or initials (the creator uses names like "Nick" and "RR"), and keep the real details they gave you - those details are what make it theirs.

If the story is too big for 60 seconds, don't squeeze it. Split it into parts and write the part that was asked for (Part 1 by default), with a title in the creator's format: **"Part 1: <title>"**, e.g. "Part 1: वो दिन जब मैं खुलकर हँसती थी". Each part should:

- Open with its own hook, so someone who missed the earlier part still stays.
- Cover one stretch of the story and stop at a moment that pulls toward the next part, without explaining it. The creator's Part 1 ends on "मैं ज़ोर-ज़ोर से हँसती थी।", calling back to its title.
- Use a call to action that points to the next part, e.g. "Part 2 कल…" or "What happened next - in Part 2.", followed by "Follow Life, Unsaid for more."

In the notes, list in one line each what the remaining parts would cover, so the creator can see the whole arc.

## Language

Write in the language the creator asks for, or the one they wrote their request in. English is the default.

For Hindi, and for any Hindi-English mix, write the way the creator does in their own scripts: **Hindi words in Devanagari, everyday English words left in English letters** - "सबके ऑफिस में love stories होती हैं।", "एक-एक घंटे का lunch break". Keep the English words people actually say (college, group, desk, normal, special feel, stress, marks) in English instead of translating them into formal Hindi, because the creator reads these scripts aloud and formal Hindi sounds like a news anchor. Use this mix even when the request itself is typed in Roman Hinglish. Write the whole script in Roman letters only if the creator asks for that.

Keep "Follow Life, Unsaid for more." in English as the brand line in every language. Write the visual prompts in English, because AI video tools follow English prompts best.

## Scenes and visuals

Split the script into **6-8 scenes** of roughly 5-10 seconds each, so the picture changes as often as Shorts viewers expect. Each scene gets its voiceover line(s) and one prompt for a realistic moving shot, made with an AI image-to-video tool such as Pippit.

Write each visual so it can be pasted into that tool as it is:

- **Realistic and cinematic**, in everyday Indian life - middle-class homes, kitchens, local trains, scooters, hostel rooms, tiffin boxes, tuition centres, hospital corridors.
- **One clear subject doing one action**, plus the setting, the light (morning window light, a tube light at night, golden hour), and a **camera movement** (slow push-in, handheld follow, static wide, rack focus) so the shot moves.
- **The same character gets the same description every time** - e.g. "the father, early 50s, grey stubble, faded blue half-sleeve shirt" - because the tool doesn't remember earlier scenes.
- **Show what the words leave out.** The visual should add something, not illustrate the line literally.
- Vertical 9:16, with no text, captions, or logos in the image.

## Output format

Use this layout so the creator can record straight from it:

```
## Hook options
A: <hook - used in the script below>
B: <alternative hook, similar length so the timing still works>

## Script: <short working title, or "Part N: <title>" for a series> · <N> words · ~<S> s

**Scene 1 · 0-6 s · Hook**
VO: <line(s)>
Visual: <prompt>

**Scene 2 · 6-13 s**
VO: ...
Visual: ...

...

**Scene N · 55-60 s · Call to action**
VO: <...> Follow Life, Unsaid for more.
Visual: <prompt>

## Full voiceover
<the whole voiceover as one block, with a line break wherever to pause, for reading aloud>

## Notes to make it better
- <2-3 specific suggestions>
```

The notes are the creator's quick feedback, so keep them short and specific to this script: which hook is stronger and why, a line to cut if it runs long, a personal detail that would make it hit harder, or where to pause.
