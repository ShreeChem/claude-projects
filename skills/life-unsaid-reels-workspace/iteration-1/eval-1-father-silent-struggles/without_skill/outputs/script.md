# Life, Unsaid | Reel Script: "Fine."
### The story behind a father's silence

| | |
|---|---|
| **Length** | 60 seconds |
| **Format** | 9:16 vertical (YouTube Shorts / Reels / WhatsApp Status) |
| **Style** | Faceless cinematic B-roll + slow, warm voiceover + burned-in captions |
| **Tone** | Quiet, tender, no melodrama. Let the details do the crying. |
| **Spoken words** | 148 (about 2.5 words per second, with breathing room for pauses) |
| **Loop** | The last word, "Fine," flows straight back into the opening line. |

---

## THE SCRIPT

### 0:00 to 0:04 | HOOK
**Visual:** Extreme close-up of a father's face in profile, lit only by a phone screen at night. He's not crying. He's just still.
**VO:** "Your father has never said he's tired. That doesn't mean he isn't."
**On-screen text:** He's never said he's tired.
**Sound:** Room silence, one ceiling fan, then the first piano note lands on "isn't."

---

### 0:04 to 0:12 | BEHIND THE BLANK FACE
**Visual:** Family dinner table. Everyone talking and laughing, out of focus. He's in focus, eating slowly, eyes somewhere else. Cut to his fingers tapping silently on the table, as if counting.
**VO:** "That blank face at dinner isn't coldness. It's a man doing math. Rent. Mom's medicines. The EMI due on the fifth."
**On-screen text:** It's not coldness. It's math.
**Sound:** Soft piano, muffled dinner chatter underneath.

---

### 0:12 to 0:19 | BEHIND THE HARD WORK
**Visual:** 5:40 AM on a wall clock. Dark house. He ties his shoelaces at the door without turning on the light. Scooter starts outside. Hard cut to night: the gate opens, the house is dark again.
**VO:** "He leaves before you wake. Comes home after you sleep. Ask about his day? One word. 'Fine.'"
**On-screen text:** "Fine."
**Sound:** Old alarm clock ticking, scooter engine starting, then a gate creak at night.

---

### 0:19 to 0:30 | BEHIND THE STRUGGLE
**Visual:** Split frame. Left: his worn shoes, the sole stitched twice. Right: a child's shiny new school shoes in a box. Then a slow push-in on him rubbing pain balm on his lower back alone, bedroom door half-closed.
**VO:** "His shoes are four years old, stitched twice. Yours were new every school year. His back has hurt for months. He skips the doctor. Your fees come first."
**On-screen text:** Your fees come first. His back can wait.
**Sound:** Piano picks up a second hand (harmony enters).

---

### 0:30 to 0:43 | BEHIND HIS SHOULDERS (THE PLANNING)
**Visual:** A drawer slides open. A plain plastic file labelled "IMPORTANT." Hands flip through it: insurance papers, a fixed deposit receipt with the child's name, and a folded handwritten note that reads, "If something happens to me..." The drawer closes. Cut to the back of his shoulders as he walks away down a corridor.
**VO:** "In a drawer you've never opened, there's a file. Insurance. A savings plan in your name. A note: 'If something happens to me.' He never showed you. He just carried it."
**On-screen text:** He never showed you. He just carried it.
**Sound:** Paper rustle. Music drops to a single held note on "carried it."

---

### 0:43 to 0:51 | THE TURN
**Visual:** Old family photo. Young father, laughing, holding a baby on his shoulders. Slow dissolve to the same man today, sitting alone on the edge of the bed, shoulders slightly bent.
**VO:** "He isn't emotionless. He just decided someone in this house had to stay strong, so everyone else could feel."
**On-screen text:** Someone had to stay strong, so everyone else could feel.
**Sound:** Full piano swell, the emotional peak.

---

### 0:51 to 1:00 | PAYOFF + CALL TO ACTION
**Visual:** A phone screen with an old chat from "Papa" that just says "Reached safely?" Then the phone lights up: *Calling Papa...* Final shot: his phone buzzing on the table, and the faintest smile as he picks it up. Hold for 1 second. Cut to black.
**VO:** "His 'I love you' always sounded like 'Reached safely?' So tonight, say it first. He'll say 'Fine.' But he'll smile."
**On-screen text:** Call him tonight. Say it first.
**End card (black screen, 1 sec):** *Life, Unsaid*: some love is never spoken, only carried.
**Sound:** Music fades. A single phone ring. Silence. (The silence makes the loop back to the hook feel seamless.)

## CLEAN VOICEOVER (for recording / teleprompter)

> Your father has never said he's tired. That doesn't mean he isn't.
>
> That blank face at dinner isn't coldness. It's a man doing math. Rent. Mom's medicines. The EMI due on the fifth.
>
> He leaves before you wake. Comes home after you sleep. Ask about his day? One word. "Fine."
>
> His shoes are four years old, stitched twice. Yours were new every school year. His back has hurt for months. He skips the doctor. Your fees come first.
>
> In a drawer you've never opened, there's a file. Insurance. A savings plan in your name. A note: "If something happens to me." He never showed you. He just carried it.
>
> He isn't emotionless. He just decided someone in this house had to stay strong, so everyone else could feel.
>
> His "I love you" always sounded like "Reached safely?" So tonight, say it first. He'll say "Fine." But he'll smile.

**Delivery notes**
- Speak low and close to the mic, like you're telling a secret, not giving a speech.
- Pause a full beat after every one-word line ("Rent." "Fine." "Insurance.").
- Don't push emotion into your voice. The calmer you sound, the harder it hits.
- Slow down a little on the last line, and smile slightly on "But he'll smile." You'll hear it in the audio.

## SHOOTING NOTES (all doable faceless, on a phone)
- **Who to film:** Your own father or uncle, shot from behind, in profile, or hands only. No face needed, and it feels more universal.
- **Light:** Warm, low light (a single lamp or tubelight from the side). Avoid bright, flat lighting; this story lives in shadows.
- **Props checklist:** worn shoes, new school shoes in a box, pain balm, a plain file marked "IMPORTANT," insurance papers or an FD receipt (blur any real numbers), a handwritten note, an old family photo, a phone showing a "Reached safely?" chat from "Papa."
- **Camera:** Slow push-ins and static shots only. No fast cuts until the 0:12 morning-to-night hard cut.
- **Captions:** Burn them in, white text with a soft shadow, lower third. Most people will watch the first pass on mute.
